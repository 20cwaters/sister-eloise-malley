About the mission

The is a call in two parts. Sister missionaries assigned to a Church historic site spend about their first four months there, and then move to the teaching mission named in their call for the rest of their service.

The first part is the Mormon Battalion Historic Site in Old Town San Diego. It tells the story of the Mormon Battalion, a group of Latter-day Saint volunteers who marched roughly 2,000 miles from Iowa to San Diego in 1846 and 1847, arriving there in January 1847. The second part is the California Oakland/San Francisco Mission, run from Oakland and covering cities around San Francisco Bay. It took its current name in 2009.

The Bay Area has a mild, Mediterranean climate: dry summers, wet winters, and summer fog that rolls in through the Golden Gate, while the valleys just over the East Bay hills run much hotter. Missionaries there teach in , and many people in the Bay Area speak other languages at home too.

How a mission works

What is the MTC?

The Missionary Training Center. Eloise began at the one in Provo, Utah, where new missionaries study the gospel and practice teaching before they leave for their missions. There is no second training center on her path: from Provo she goes straight to California.

What is a transfer?

Roughly every six weeks the mission president reassigns missionaries. She may stay put or be moved to a new city, and she may get a new companion either way. Transfer news usually shows up in the weekly email, and the Where She Is page gets updated when it does.

What is a companion?

Missionaries are always paired up. Companions live, study, and teach together for the length of a transfer or longer. You will see a lot of names come and go over the course of a mission.

What is a P-day?

Preparation day — one day a week for laundry, groceries, sightseeing, and writing home. That is when the weekly email arrives, and when she is allowed to call or video chat with her family.
